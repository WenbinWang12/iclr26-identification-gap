\section{A Two-Constraint Method Under a Fixed Budget}
\label{sec:method}

Section~\ref{sec:gap-real} shows that the identification gap is a real, separable
term at the output layer, and that one offset per scope recovers $+1.51$~pp of it
over a single global offset. Section~\ref{sec:three-anchors} shows that three
parameter-space interventions on the \emph{representation} layer leave that output
term untouched. Read together they say the fixed budget faces two capacity
constraints that live in different places (Section~\ref{sec:setup}): the output
layer cannot serve every scope-mate with one offset, and the representation cannot
hold fifteen tasks' worth of change in one shared adapter. This section proposes a
method that spends the same budget to relieve \emph{both}, and measures the four
corners of the design at an identical parameter count.

\paragraph{The two moves.}
The output-layer move is the per-scope offset of Section~\ref{sec:gap-real}: read
the prompt's option list, look up its scope, and add the offset that best serves
that scope's member tasks. It has zero rank and costs a small per-scope table
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}, $33$ floats on this stream), and it reads no task index.
The representation-layer move changes \emph{allocation} rather than the objective.
A single shared rank-$R$ adapter must absorb every task, so late tasks overwrite
the subspace early tasks needed. Instead, partition the tasks into $K$ semantic
families and give each family its own rank-$R/K$ adapter. The $K$ adapters hold
exactly the parameters of one shared rank-$R$ adapter ($K\times R/K = R$), verified
per arm at run time, so this is a reallocation and not extra capacity. Each family
adapter sees only its family's tasks, so cross-family interference is contained by
construction rather than penalised after the fact --- which is what
Section~\ref{sec:three-anchors}'s penalties tried and failed to do.

\paragraph{The target the offset move approximates is a Chebyshev centre.}
The output move is not arbitrary: the best single offset for a scope is a
minimax object, and naming it tells us both what a deployable rule must estimate
and where the term must vanish. Fix a scope $S$ with labels
$\ell_1,\dots,\ell_{K_S}$. For each member task $g\in\mathrm{mem}(S)$, let
$\bz_g\in\R^{K_S}$ be an offset on those labels that maximises $g$'s balanced
accuracy, gauge-fixed to zero mean --- adding a constant to every coordinate
leaves the restricted argmax unchanged, so only $P_0\bz_g$ is identified. A single
shared offset $\bb$ must serve all members at once.

\begin{proposition}[Logit-space minimax; unconditional]
\label{prop:cheb}
Let $Z_S=\{P_0\bz_g : g\in\mathrm{mem}(S)\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\bb\in\R^{K_S}}\ \max_{g\in\mathrm{mem}(S)}\ \|\bb-P_0\bz_g\|_2
  \;=\; r_S,
  \label{eq:cheb}
\end{equation}
where $r_S$ is the Chebyshev radius of $Z_S$, the minimum is attained at the
unique Chebyshev centre of $Z_S$, and $r_S\ge\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{diam}(Z_S)$.
Moreover $r_S=q_1(S)$, the $m=1$ quantization radius of Section~\ref{sec:ladder}.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cheb} is stated in logit space and holds with no
assumptions. We are deliberate about what it does \emph{not} say: the
corresponding statement in accuracy space, that some task must lose an amount
proportional to $r_S$, requires a margin-density constant $\kappa$ and is false
without it --- two tasks with margins $\pm10$ can be rescaled to margins
$\pm 0.01$, shrinking every distance by $1000\times$ while the accuracy loss stays
at $1$. Distance and loss are different quantities. We report the measured
$\kappa$ wherever the accuracy-space form is used, and its worst value on this
stream is $0.0182$, on WiC. Section~\ref{sec:three-anchors} contains a
counterexample from our own runs in which $q_1$ and $q_2$ both shrank while
accuracy fell, so this caveat is load-bearing rather than pro forma.

A feature of \eqref{eq:cheb} organises the whole design: the target offset depends
on the member tasks' \emph{optima} and on nothing else --- not their examples,
their logits, or their order. The offset arm we score in
Section~\ref{sec:combined} fits those optima against the \emph{final} model from
each task's held-out \texttt{risk} split, disjoint from the scored \texttt{audit}
split. This is an upper bound in the same sense that oracle scope routing is: it
reads each past task's optimum at the current model. Two questions of
\emph{deployability} then remain, and we treat them as such rather than folding
them into the headline. First, where a task-free rule can get current-model
estimates of those optima without re-reading old examples; the boundary condition
below makes the term itself falsifiable, and Section~\ref{sec:enumeration}
enumerates the admissible sources. Second, whether grouping changes the picture
by construction: because a family adapter only ever moves when its own family's
tasks arrive, a stored per-family optimum drifts less than one stored against a
monolithic adapter every later task perturbs --- a mechanism we motivate here and
flag in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} as measured only at the upper bound.

\paragraph{A boundary condition that makes the output term falsifiable.}
If tasks' verbalizer sets are pairwise disjoint, one offset serves every task and
$\Delta^{\mathrm{id}}\equiv0$ exactly (Proposition~\ref{prop:disjoint}). The
output-layer move can only help where output tokens are \emph{shared}; on Order-4
ten of fifteen tasks share theirs, and we verify the zero prediction in every run
($0$ violations, worst deviation $0.0$). A stream of pairwise-disjoint verbalizers
is one where our method reduces to grouping alone, and we say so.

\subsection{The admissible sources for the offset, and no third}
\label{sec:enumeration}

The offset arm we score is an upper bound because it reads each task's optimum at
the current model. A \emph{deployable} rule must produce a scope's offset using
only (i) bounded state carried across task boundaries and (ii) the query itself.
Any estimate of $\bz_g$ is fitted from logits, and logits require a model and
examples. Crossing the two gives Table~\ref{tab:admissible}.

\noindent The first row is the upper bound scored in Section~\ref{sec:combined};
the last is the no-offset floor. The two admissible non-vacuous rows differ in
exactly one respect: which argument to $\mathrm{acc}_g(\theta,\bb)$ they keep
current. Storing the optima (SSO) keeps the examples and lets the model go stale;
computing from the query batch (BPO) keeps the model and gives up the examples. No
admissible rule keeps both, because keeping both is rehearsal. Both deployable
approximations are imperfect on this stream --- SSO loses to staleness
(Section~\ref{sec:sso}), BPO to a class-balance confound
(Section~\ref{sec:bpo}) --- which is why the offset column of
Section~\ref{sec:combined}'s $2\times2$ is reported as an upper bound and the
deployment gap is stated plainly in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. The
representation move carries the analogous caveat: routing to the right family
adapter uses the scope at evaluation, an upper bound over a task-free router. What
the $2\times2$ establishes cleanly is the \emph{allocation} claim --- that at a
fixed budget the two moves each recover accuracy and compose --- independent of how
tightly the deployable versions track it.

\paragraph{Why storing the optima goes stale (SSO), and how grouping bears on it.}
Maintain a table indexed by scope. When training on task $g$ finishes, fit $\bz_g$
on $g$'s own \texttt{risk} split using $g$'s logits \emph{at that moment},
gauge-fix to zero mean, append $P_0\bz_g$ to the entry for $\mathrm{scope}(g)$, and
discard the logits and examples. At inference, look up the query's scope and apply
the Chebyshev centre of its stored optima. This is admissible --- it touches only
the task being trained, keys on the option list not a task index, and leaves the
objective and trainable scalars bit-identical to the no-offset arm --- with stored
state
\begin{equation}
  \textstyle\sum_S |\mathrm{table}[S]|\cdot(K_S-1)
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
scalars, $12$ entries and $\mathbf{33}$ floats on this stream, asserted by test.
Its risk, stated before we measured it: the fit uses $g$'s logits at the time $g$
was trained, and a monolithic adapter keeps moving afterwards, so a stored optimum
goes stale. Section~\ref{sec:sso} reports it costs $5.08$~pp below the no-offset
floor, with the singleton probe --- where the Chebyshev centre of a one-element set
\emph{is} that element, ruling out aggregation, routing, and gauge --- carrying
$-4.18$~pp of it. This is precisely the failure the grouping move is positioned to
soften: a family adapter is perturbed only by its own family's tasks, so a
per-family stored optimum drifts less than one stored against an adapter every
later task moves. We measure grouping and the offset each at their upper bound in
Section~\ref{sec:combined} and mark the de-staling of stored offsets under
grouping as the natural next measurement (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), not a
result we claim here.

\paragraph{Why the query-batch source fails its own control (BPO).}
Give up stored optima and read the only current-model logits an admissible rule
may touch --- the query batch's own. Section~\ref{sec:bpo} states the rule: it
gains $+4.40$~pp over the global offset and then inverts to $-0.67$~pp once the
query class mix is skewed to $70{:}30$, failing the control we pre-registered
against our own splits. Three separately pre-registered rules occupy this row and
all three fail: BPO on its control; a separation-gated invariant offset (SIO) on
all six of its frozen criteria (the two-component structure it assumes is absent
--- the class margin $s=z_1-z_0$ is unimodal, median separation $0.89$ SD --- and
the gate abstains on $78.6\%$ of batches); and a variance-gated rule (VGC) that
passes on the development seed and reverses out of sample (worst-case $-9.38$~pp
against a $-3.0$ floor). A fourth rule (SCG) gating on the magnitude of its own
proposed correction fails its worst-case criterion by $1.34$~pp on $2$ of $350$
held-out batches. What survives is a measured asymmetry --- batch calibration gains
$+5.40$~pp on average but loses up to $14.84$~pp, precisely on batches where the
oracle offset has little to gain --- and one confirmed mechanism: within each task,
the realised gain is predicted by the magnitude of the proposed correction (median
rank correlation $+0.72$, positive in $8$ of $8$ tasks, five seeds used to fit
nothing). A single global threshold on that magnitude is not enough to make the
row safe out of sample. So the query-batch source is a negative result we can now
attribute, which is why the method deploys the stored-optima source and reports its
upper bound honestly.

\paragraph{More than one centre per scope buys nothing here.}
Allowing $m$ centres per scope replaces the Chebyshev centre with a minimax
$m$-centre codebook of radius $q_m(S)$ and needs a task-free router. We implement
it for the stored-optima source with an honest prototype router (nearest stored
class-mean in encoder state, no task index): it buys $+0.52$~pp with a CI spanning
zero, matching Section~\ref{sec:gap-real}'s finding that the codebook gained
$0.0000$ over $m=1$. Every primary number in this paper is an $m=1$ number, which
needs no router at all.
