\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Part of what looks like capacity exhaustion in continual PEFT is not. On a
$15$-task stream under one fixed LoRA adapter, an additive per-label offset on the
verbalizer logits --- zero rank, a handful of scalars --- recovers accuracy that
rank-allocation methods are built to protect. We isolated that component, showed it
is not reachable from parameter space by three separate routes, and then found that
both ways of deploying it fail --- each for a reason the design let us measure
rather than guess at.

Three results, in the order the evidence forces, and then the one route they leave.

\emph{The component is real and its information content is specific.} Under one
globally shared offset the identification gap is $3.13$~pp; under an offset shared
only within a verbalizer scope it is $0.00$~pp, CI $[-1.71,+1.71]$. Scope --- free
at inference, readable from the prompt --- recovers $+1.51$~pp, CI
$[+0.43,+2.74]$. Task identity adds $+1.10$~pp, CI $[+0.40,+1.83]$, on top when
restricted to multi-task scopes where the comparison is measurable
(Section~\ref{sec:gap-real}, disclosure~(iii)). The gap is
also exactly zero when verbalizer sets are disjoint
(Proposition~\ref{prop:disjoint}), which we verify in every run and which explains
why the per-task-head literature cannot see this term at all.

\emph{Three parameter-space interventions do not touch it.} An orthogonality
penalty of O-LoRA's form, verbalizer-logit anchoring, and gauge-fixed anchoring:
none improved task-agnostic accuracy, two reduced it at the strength their own
prefix gate selected, and the one derived from the correct symmetry argument did
the most damage while the per-scope offset kept recovering the same $+1.51$~pp on
top of each. The harm is a property of the intervention \emph{at the gated}
$\lambda$: our $\lambda_a=0.5$ arm, completed to three seeds, fails the same
criterion from the other side with no detectable effect.

\emph{Storing the offsets is worse than storing nothing.} A rehearsal-free
streaming table --- fit each task's optimum at its boundary, apply the scope's
Chebyshev centre at test time, $33$ floats --- scores $5.08$~pp \emph{below} using
no offset at all and damages $11$ of $12$ tasks. Singleton scopes locate the cause
without ambiguity: there the Chebyshev centre of a one-element table \emph{is} that
element, so aggregation, routing, and the gauge cannot contribute, and those scopes
still lose $4.18$~pp, CI $[-6.61,-1.76]$. The residue is staleness, measured at
$6.16$~pp mean. So the $+1.51$~pp is real but reachable only by fitting against the
\emph{current} model: it is available to rehearsal and not to offset storage.

That last result is the one we would most like to have gone the other way, and it
determines what we can and cannot claim. We cannot claim a deployable
rehearsal-free method for this term. We can claim that the term exists, that it is
worth roughly $1.5$~pp on this stream, that three parameter-space families do not
address it, and that two specific output-layer constructions fail with identified
mechanisms rather than unexplained ones --- storing the offsets fails to drift, and
computing them from the query batch works only on a balanced evaluation set.

\paragraph{The second admissible rule, and why it closes rather than opens.}
Section~\ref{sec:enumeration}'s enumeration has two non-vacuous rows, and storing
the optima was the first. The second keeps the model current and gives up the
stored examples, which admits exactly one source of information: the queries
themselves. Section~\ref{sec:bpo} measures it. An offset chosen to flatten the
predicted label histogram --- storing zero floats, using no labels, with no
hyperparameter --- gains $\mathbf{+4.40}$~pp over a refit global offset, clearing
the primary criterion by a wide margin.

The criterion we had pre-registered against our own confound then fails. Our audit
splits are class-balanced by construction, so a rule that equalises predicted class
frequencies is partly right for reasons belonging to our splits. Re-scored on a
$70{:}30$ query mix the gain becomes $\mathbf{-0.67}$~pp: the effect does not
attenuate off balance, it \emph{inverts}. Two further readings point the same way
--- the rule scores above the per-task oracle that should bound it, and its
advantage scales with how much of the evaluation set is visible at once, both
signatures of transductive access rather than of a decision rule. We therefore
report it as an artefact of our evaluation protocol and not as a method, which is
what that protocol's predeclared outcome instructed us to do in this exact case.

This is why we describe the design space as closed rather than unexplored, and the
qualifier matters: closed \emph{as enumerated}. Both admissible rows of
Section~\ref{sec:enumeration} reach the $1.51$~pp only by reading something a
deployed model will not have --- a value fitted before the drift, or the class
composition of the evaluation batch itself --- and the inadmissible row reads
earlier tasks' examples outright. What we have not ruled out is a rule outside our
constraint set: one that alters the training objective in a way our three failed
anchors did not, or a per-example router, which Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows can
in principle exceed even the per-task oracle. That is a stronger statement than
``we tried and failed'', and a weaker one than ``impossible'' --- it is a mechanism
for each failure and a boundary drawn where our measurements can support it.

\paragraph{Scope.}
One stream (Order-4), one backbone (T5-large), one budget (LoRA $r{=}8$,
$2.36$M trainable scalars), three seeds, generative PEFT with a shared decoder and
restricted-argmax scoring. Every comparison is internal: arms differ in one
component at an identical budget with identical splits and seeds. We ran no
published continual-PEFT baseline and make no claim against one, and the official
test files were never opened, so our absolute accuracies do not belong in
leaderboard tables. The budgeted quantization result is proved for binary shared
verbalizers and remains open beyond them.

\paragraph{The two sensitivities we said would matter, run and negative.}
We flagged two measurements as more valuable than another intervention: how the
identification gap scales with the number of tasks sharing a verbalizer, and
whether the staleness grows with stream length as the mechanism predicts. Both are
answerable from the runs already reported, so we froze criteria for them and
answered them (Phase-2R, zero additional compute). \textbf{Neither is established
on this stream.} The gap against live scope membership gives $-0.278$~pp per member,
CI $[-1.721,+1.017]$ --- sign not interpretable, interval spanning zero, and
consistent in sign across all three arms. Staleness against age gives $+0.387$~pp
per subsequent task, CI $[-0.051,+3.379]$: predicted sign, criterion failed. The
wiring check that guards both passes exactly ($87$ observations, $0$ violations).
So the claim we are left with is a level and not a rate, and this is not in tension
with Section~\ref{sec:theory}: at $\kappa\approx0.018$ that section already declared
the accuracy-space bound weak on this benchmark, and a scale dependence we cannot
resolve is what a weak bound predicts.

What remains genuinely open is therefore sharper than before: whether any
correction fixed at a task boundary survives an arbitrarily long stream. Our
evidence locates the decay in the stored value itself and measures its level, but
this stream is too short, and its per-scope membership too small, to measure how
that decay scales. Answering it needs a longer stream with more scope-mates per
verbalizer, not a new method.
